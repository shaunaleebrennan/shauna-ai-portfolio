# Brazil in Red

[← All selected work](../README.md#selected-work)

**Turning curiosity into a story people can explore**

I wanted to understand how Brazil voted, what changed between rounds, and how the picture differed for Brazilians abroad. That curiosity became Brazil in Red: an interactive atlas built with AI.

## What needed to change

Election results arrive as headlines, tables, and scattered reports. I wanted to bring the national picture, regional differences, and overseas vote into one experience. The challenge was to make a complex dataset approachable while preserving the distinctions that matter: vote share versus vote count, a change in winner versus a change in margin, and the different candidate fields in each round.

## My part in it

I set the questions, directed the research and AI-assisted build, and shaped the editorial structure and visual experience. I pushed the work through repeated reviews of maps, chart labels, comparisons, and page hierarchy. When a percentage was ambiguous, I asked for its meaning to be explicit. When selected countries obscured the bigger picture, I asked for regional context. I also expanded the story beyond the results to include people helping each other reach the polls. The resulting experience combines guided storytelling with maps and tables readers can explore for themselves.

## What came out of it

I published a bilingual atlas with English and Portuguese views, maps covering 27 Brazilian federal units, comparisons across 31 selected overseas countries and territories, and controls for exploring rounds, regions, and locations. It brings together the numbers, political context, and stories of participation, with source links and methodology notes. The project demonstrates how I take a question from research through product direction, AI-assisted implementation, and public delivery.

> AI made it faster to build. Judgment made it worth exploring.
