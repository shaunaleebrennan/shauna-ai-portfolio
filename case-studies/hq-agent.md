**Customer-led GTM for the next AI investment**

I shaped HQ Agent’s customer-led GTM, positioning, and commercial story around a substantial expansion opportunity: helping existing Workvivo customers see where it added value alongside the AI and enterprise systems they already owned.

## A growth opportunity inside the customer base

HQ Agent opened a new paid expansion motion across Workvivo’s existing customers, with a multi-million-dollar pipeline opportunity identified in internal planning. The first priority was a **customer-led GTM motion**: understand what customers needed, what they already owned, and where everyday employee work still broke down.

Many customers already had something they considered similar. The commercial challenge was to earn another investment by establishing a meaningful gap in their current approach, then showing how HQ Agent could address it.

**The buying question:** “We already have something similar. Why do we need HQ Agent?”

## Start with what the customer already has

The discovery work connected employee use cases to the customer’s AI maturity, technology environment, stakeholders, and commercial priorities. Field feedback showed different starting points, from organisations using established AI assistants to customers still building their employee experience foundations.

The two-wave Wynter study reinforced the installed-base reality: 42 of 45 participants reported Microsoft Copilot active in their organisation. The case for HQ Agent needed to explain incremental value within an existing stack.

## Compete beyond the employee experience category

The competitive set extended to **Microsoft, Workday, ServiceNow, and Salesforce**. Existing relationships and embedded capabilities made those vendors part of the buying decision, alongside specialist assistants and internal builds.

I shaped the competitive story around the employee journey and the work left for the employee to do. The seller needed to establish whether the right motion was to complement the existing investment, improve the experience around it, or compete for the use case.

### Three ways to earn a role

1. **Complement.** Show the employee need that remains unmet alongside the tools the customer already owns.
2. **Optimise.** Connect existing systems through an employee-facing experience, reducing the navigation and effort around them.
3. **Compete.** Compare the actual use case, reach, experience, and commercial trade-offs where alternatives overlap.

The competitive enablement connected the motion to customer maturity and the existing stack.

## Make build versus buy a business decision

I developed the story around the cost of inaction and the full ownership of an AI experience. Build versus buy needed to account for delivery, governance, maintenance, and operating costs, as well as the initial demonstration.

- Keep the current approach. Establish what the employee still has to search for, piece together, or ask someone else to complete. Make the remaining friction concrete.
- Build internally. Examine implementation resources, accountability for answers and actions, runtime costs, ongoing maintenance, and delivery across the workforce.
- Buy HQ Agent. Demonstrate the relevant employee journey, supported connections, governance, and desktop/mobile experience. Compare those requirements with the customer’s build or incumbent.

## Equip the customer expansion motion

I brought the work together across positioning, portfolio strategy, competitive guidance, pricing and packaging, and seller narrative. The customer upsell story was simple: the digital HQ customers already used could now do more for their people.

Seller enablement connected discovery, use cases, objections, and the role of Zoom to the same commercial story. The commercial aim was to turn an existing employee experience relationship into a credible next investment in HQ Agent.

### A customer-led path to the next investment

1. **Discover the gap.** Start with the employee need, existing investment, stakeholders, and success criteria.
2. **Demonstrate incremental value.** Show a relevant journey through trusted answers, company understanding, and supported action.
3. **Make the buying case.** Connect the alternative, ownership costs, packaging, proof, and implementation considerations.

Customer discovery → differentiated value → a commercial decision.
