# HQ Agent

[← All selected work](../README.md#selected-work)

**Customer-led GTM for the next AI investment**

I shaped a customer-led expansion motion for HQ Agent, combining call insights and competitive research with a clear positioning decision: an employee experience agent that helps people find answers and get everyday workplace tasks done.

## A growth opportunity inside the customer base

HQ Agent opened a new paid expansion motion across Workvivo’s existing customers, with a multi-million-dollar pipeline opportunity identified in internal planning. The first priority was a **customer-led GTM motion**: understand what customers needed, what they already owned, and where everyday employee work still broke down.

Many customers already had something they considered similar. The commercial challenge was to earn another investment by establishing a meaningful gap in their current approach, then showing how HQ Agent could address it.

**The buying question:** “We already have something similar. Why do we need HQ Agent?”

## Let the customer and the alternatives shape the GTM

I combined **ZRA (Zoom Revenue Accelerator) call insights**, desk research on Microsoft, Workday, ServiceNow, Salesforce, and other competitors, and buyer message testing. Each source answered a different question.

Customer conversations exposed objections and existing investments. Desk research examined how adjacent platforms positioned their capabilities. Wynter helped assess reactions to specific messages. Together, those inputs informed the use cases, competitive story, and seller guidance.

### Three inputs. Different jobs.

1. **Customer conversations / ZRA.** Understand the customer’s use cases, existing stack, objections, and buying questions.
2. **Competitor desk research.** Examine adjacent alternatives, their positioning, and the reasons a customer might default to them.
3. **Wynter message testing.** Assess reactions to positioning statements and the proof buyers wanted.

Call evidence, competitor analysis, and message testing informed different parts of the GTM.

## Define the job of an employee experience agent

A major part of the messaging work was clarifying what HQ Agent actually was. Enterprise search described a capability; “agent” alone left the purpose unclear. I positioned it as an **employee experience agent**, focused on trusted organisational answers and everyday workplace tasks.

I used Gartner’s four work modes to make the distinction concrete: productivity assistants support the work people are hired to do; HQ Agent helps remove the friction around being ready and able to do it. The mapping also clarified the role of Workvivo HQ and ZoomMate.

In the October messaging review, the commercial team reported that this framing was resonating with customers and helping them understand HQ Agent’s role alongside Copilot.

### Different work. Different role for AI.

| Work mode | Employee need | Positioning role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work | Core tasks people are hired to do. Create, analyse, research, and deliver core work. | Productivity AI / ZoomMate |
| Work to do work | Supporting activities around the core job. Find relevant knowledge and reduce searching, switching, and chasing. | HQ Agent |
| Work to enable work | The workplace services needed to function in the organisation. Policy answers and supported routine HR, IT, and workplace requests. | HQ Agent |
| Work to feel connected | The social and organisational experience of work. Stay informed, participate, and feel connected. | Workvivo HQ |

Adapted from Gartner’s four work modes. Labels follow our seller deck; the product mapping is our positioning application.

## Compete beyond the employee experience category

The competitive set extended to **Microsoft, Workday, ServiceNow, and Salesforce**. Existing relationships and embedded capabilities made those vendors part of the buying decision, alongside specialist assistants and internal builds.

I shaped the competitive story around the employee journey and the work left for the employee to do. The seller needed to establish whether the right motion was to complement the existing investment, improve the experience around it, or compete for the use case.

### Three ways to earn a role

1. **Complement.** Show the employee need that remains unmet alongside the tools the customer already owns.
2. **Optimise.** Connect existing systems through an employee-facing experience, reducing the navigation and effort around them.
3. **Compete.** Compare the actual use case, reach, experience, and commercial trade-offs where alternatives overlap.

The competitive enablement connected the motion to customer maturity and the existing stack.

## Make build versus buy a business decision

I developed the story around the cost of inaction and the full ownership of an AI experience. Build versus buy needed to account for delivery, governance, maintenance, and operating costs, as well as the initial demonstration.

- Keep the current approach. Establish what the employee still has to search for, piece together, or ask someone else to complete. Make the remaining friction concrete.
- Build internally. Examine implementation resources, accountability for answers and actions, runtime costs, ongoing maintenance, and delivery across the workforce.
- Buy HQ Agent. Demonstrate the relevant employee journey, supported connections, governance, and desktop/mobile experience. Compare those requirements with the customer’s build or incumbent.

## Make the Zoom relationship meaningful to the buyer

I worked with Product and Zoom’s AI teams to connect the technology to the commercial story. The better-together message needed to explain why the foundation and context mattered to an employee’s answer or task.

The dynamic-context story brought company knowledge together with relevant updates, decisions, and communications in Workvivo. The federated-model story explained Zoom’s use of multiple models. I translated those ideas into buyer value and seller language, keeping product availability and supported sources explicit.

### From architecture to a reason to care

1. **Company knowledge.** Policies, documents, and connected knowledge provide the established information.
2. **Dynamic context.** Relevant company communications help explain what has changed and why it matters.
3. **Zoom’s federated AI foundation.** Translate the use of multiple models into a discussion of task fit, performance, and enterprise AI economics.

Established knowledge + current company communication + the Zoom AI foundation.

## Turn technical capability into a commercial progression

The employee value progression became **“From search, to answer, to job done.”** It connected retrieval, grounded answers, and supported task execution to recognisable customer needs.

I co-developed pricing and packaging with the Revenue leader, considering customer value, AI maturity, competitive alternatives, use cases, and sustainable economics. Trust messaging made permissions, grounding, citations, and supported actions part of evaluation.

Search → Answer → Action

## Equip the customer expansion motion

I brought the work together across employee experience agent positioning, portfolio strategy, competitive guidance, pricing and packaging, and seller narrative. The customer upsell story was simple: the digital HQ customers already used could now do more for their people.

Seller enablement connected discovery, use cases, objections, and the role of Zoom to the same commercial story. The commercial aim was to turn an existing employee experience relationship into a credible next investment in HQ Agent.

### A customer-led path to the next investment

1. **Discover the gap.** Start with the employee need, existing investment, stakeholders, and success criteria.
2. **Demonstrate incremental value.** Show a relevant journey through trusted answers, company understanding, and supported action.
3. **Make the buying case.** Connect the alternative, ownership costs, packaging, proof, and implementation considerations.

Customer discovery → differentiated value → a commercial decision.
