**Commercialising enterprise AI**

Turning enterprise search, trusted answers, self-service, and agentic action into one credible product story—and a launch buyers could navigate.

**What this demonstrates:** Agentic AI positioning, portfolio architecture, trust messaging, packaging, launch orchestration, and enterprise sales enablement.

## Context

HQ Agent extended Workvivo beyond communications and employee experience into trusted enterprise answers, search, self-service, and agentic task execution. It sat at the intersection of enterprise knowledge, RAG, permissions, employee experience, and Zoom’s broader AI technology.

## The challenge

The launch could not be reduced to ‘better enterprise search.’ It needed a clear category, a credible trust story, an understandable relationship with Workvivo HQ and ZoomMate, and sustainable packaging for different levels of AI value.

## Strategic insight

The most useful commercial progression was SEARCH → ANSWER → ACTION: from finding information, to receiving a grounded answer, to increasingly completing the task. The launch idea became: From search, to answer, to job done.

## My contribution

- Product and portfolio positioning
- AI maturity story and competitive framing
- Pricing and packaging co-developed with the Revenue leader
- Seller narrative and enablement
- Translation of trust, permissions, citations, and grounding into buyer value
- GTM execution across Product, Revenue, and the market

## Key decisions

- Market the buyer value of RAG—not the acronym
- Use ‘grounded, cited, and permission-aware’ instead of unsafe absolute claims
- Differentiate HQ Agent’s employee support from ZoomMate’s deeper productivity use cases
- Price levels of AI value rather than charging for novelty

## Outcome

The result was a portfolio and launch narrative designed to make a technically complex enterprise AI product easier to understand, sell, and evaluate. Shauna shaped the commercial story; she did not engineer the underlying AI system.

## What I learned

Technical depth matters in Product Marketing when it changes the claim, the value, the packaging, or the buyer’s trust.
