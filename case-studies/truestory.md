# Truestory

[← All selected work](../README.md#selected-work)

**The narrative system for go-to-market teams**

The release note is right. The deck is from last quarter. And an assistant just wrote the next email from both. Nobody lied. Nobody checked. Truestory keeps one approved record of what a company can say, shows where every line is used, and fixes it when the product moves.

## Why I'm building it

I've spent my career as the person who owns the story. Every release lands the same job on product marketing: work out what changed, what sales can now say, and which slides are quietly wrong.

That job used to be slow. Now it's risky too. Every assistant repeats whatever it was given, right or wrong, at scale. The more content a team makes, the more it matters that one approved version of the truth sits underneath it.

### Where a pitch goes stale

Product ships a change → The release note is right → The deck and battlecard are not → An assistant repeats the old line

## The bet: own the job, not a category

I mapped two positioning bets and scored each on market fit, differentiation, room in the market and whether I could execute. The stronger one doesn't fight for a crowded AI category. It goes after how teams do this today.

- A job, not a shelf. Keeping what sales says true after the product changes.
- The status quo. A shared doc, a busy PMM, an assistant and a checklist.
- Proof, not promises. Only approved wording passes, a roadmap item is never sold as shipped, and a named person approves every change.

## Three decisions that shaped the product

The build is fast because the rules came first. Three choices did the most work.

- Truth first, fit second. Wording passes a strict gate, then gets ranked for the reader and the moment. A great fit never brings back an unapproved line.
- AI proposes, people approve. Assistants can draft, comment and suggest. Only a named person can approve, and every decision goes into a history that shows if anything was changed.
- One source, many destinations. The record lives in Git. Decks, pages, RFP answers and assistants all draw from it, so a fix made once shows up everywhere it's registered.

## Truestory runs on Truestory

Truestory's own messaging is a record of approved claims, each with its evidence. A test fails if the messaging guide says anything the record doesn't. Propose calling the trade mark 'registered' and the system holds it: the application is pending, so the claim isn't true yet.

I'm the product owner and orchestrator. Claude is the builder. I set the strategy, the rules and the bar; every change ships through a reviewed pull request.
