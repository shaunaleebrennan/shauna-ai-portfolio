**Repositioning a category for the AI era**

Repositioning employee experience for the AI era—without losing the adoption, trust, and culture that made the platform distinctive.

**What this demonstrates:** Platform repositioning, category evolution, executive narrative, launch strategy, and buyer storytelling across IT, HR, and Comms.

## Context

Workvivo had established itself as an Employee Experience Platform. As enterprise software moved into an AI-native era, the challenge was bigger than adding AI features to the existing story: the company needed a credible new ambition without abandoning the employee-experience equity that made it distinctive.

## The challenge

What should Workvivo become when AI changes how employees interact with information, systems, and work? A generic ‘we’re now an AI company’ claim would have discarded the adoption, culture, frontline reach, and trust the platform had already earned.

## Strategic insight

AI becomes more valuable when it lives inside an employee experience people already use. The platform story shifted toward an AI-native headquarters for every employee, captured in the central idea: Give work a home.

## My contribution

- Strategic positioning and category narrative
- Messaging architecture across IT, HR, and Comms buyers
- Launch strategy, seller narrative, and website story
- Analyst story and competitive framing
- Portfolio narrative connecting Workvivo HQ to the broader AI strategy

## Key decisions

- Position AI as embedded in the employee experience—not bolted on
- Make adoption part of the AI ROI story
- Frame one destination where employees can understand, find, and increasingly act
- Keep frontline employees inside the enterprise AI narrative

## Outcome

Workvivo publicly launched HQ while serving 9 million users and after surpassing $100M ARR. Those are company proof points during Shauna’s tenure, not outcomes attributed to her alone.

## What I learned

Repositioning works when it expands what a brand can mean without erasing the reason customers trusted it in the first place.
