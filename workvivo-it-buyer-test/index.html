Product context and how to use it

Workvivo: the company and master brand. Workvivo HQ: the platform experience bringing communication, knowledge, engagement and action together. HQ Agent: an AI product within that experience for retrieving knowledge and helping employees take action. Keep the platform, included capabilities and separately purchased add-ons distinct. Confirm the exact workflow, integrations, controls and commercial scope before promising them.

Regional discovery: use the selected market to explore countries, employee groups, languages, AI ownership and local requirements. Europe, North America and Asia Pacific remain distinct market options. EMEA is an additional angle when useful: clarify the specific countries and any data-handling or workforce consultation requirements. Market focus provides context; it does not change the score.

Make the platform case, then qualify the agent opportunity

The HQ conversation: where employees receive communication, find knowledge, connect with the organisation and access everyday work. Explore fragmented destinations, difficult navigation and the effort involved in getting people to adopt change.

The HQ Agent conversation: a defined need for AI retrieval or supported task execution. Establish the use case and confirm the package rather than implying every agent capability comes with the platform.

For existing customers: explain the platform value and confirmed entitlements first. Discuss any additional purchase separately, with its own use case and success measures. Confirm account-specific scope and timing with the customer team.

“Isn’t this just an intranet?” Ask what their intranet already does well and where the employee journey breaks. Demonstrate the relevant communication, knowledge or action journey and how it fits the current stack; avoid defining the category by what competitors supposedly cannot do.

Bring the right buying group into the conversation

Comms and HR: employee needs, communication and engagement. Digital workplace and IT: navigation, system fit, identity, governance and support. Transformation and change leaders: adoption and sustained behaviour change. Operations and knowledge owners: process friction, content quality and ownership.

Discovery question: “Who owns the employee experience across these systems, and who is accountable when employees cannot find what they need or complete the next step?”

Agree a shared outcome before a feature tour. For a platform evaluation, measure findability, reach, repeat use or administration effort. For an agent evaluation, add task completion, answer quality, controls and escalation.

Start with frontline reach and completed work

Ask where the organisation’s AI investment is reaching employees today. Which frontline or mobile workers still struggle to find an answer or complete a request? Make that gap the reason to involve the AI owner.

Opening question: “Where are employees still relying on a manager or service desk to get routine work done, even with the AI tools you already have?”

Message direction: connect employee access to a measurable IT outcome: time to complete a request, successful self-service, repeat use or avoidable support demand. Establish a baseline before promising improvement.

Explain search, answer and action precisely

Search: find relevant information. Answer: help the employee understand what applies. Action: complete a supported task in the relevant system. Show where the proposed journey begins and ends.

Illustrative discovery journey: find a leave policy → check personal eligibility or balance → submit a request → receive confirmation. This is a scoping example, not a promise that every step or system is supported.

Technical questions: does this step need indexed documents or a live system lookup? Which connector and end-user licence are required? Which permissions apply to reading and taking action? Where is confirmation needed, and what happens if the action fails?

Build a credible “why HQ Agent?” conversation

Find a gap in the current employee journey before comparing products. Ask which workers already use their copilot, search or service portal successfully and which tasks remain unfinished. Evaluate HQ Agent against that specific gap.

“We already have AI.” “Which employee groups and everyday requests does it serve well today? Where is there still friction?” Coexistence does not establish that HQ Agent can control or route work through another AI assistant.

“Why should IT own this?” Bring the workflow owner and AI owner into the same discussion: the business team defines the need; IT evaluates system fit, access, support and measurable value.

“How do we trust it?” Ask for evidence of source grounding, permission handling, action boundaries, data processing and failure recovery for the exact workflow. General assurances do not answer those questions.

Next step: a scoped workflow demonstration with a solution specialist, followed by a pilot with agreed success measures. Use current approved commercial terms for cost comparisons.

      Six IT buyer questions

      A test of whether the message helps an IT buyer understand, evaluate or defend a technology decision.

      • What business problem forces action?
      • How does this affect the wider system?
      • Can I defend the value internally?
      • What will implementation really require?
      • What evidence reduces the risk?
      • Is the message specific and honest?
      Stage-aware: Discover content should define the problem and consequences. Evaluate content must explain fit and provide proof. Commit content must support financial justification, implementation confidence and consensus.
